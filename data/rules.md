# Blades in the Void

System reference document. Draft 2.

---

## 1. Characters

A character has:

- **Trademarks**: broad descriptors, one from each category below. A **Past** trademark is what the character did before joining the crew. A **Present** trademark is the role they play on jobs. A **Perk** trademark is a notable advantage: an ability, quality, possession, or place.
- **Edges**: narrow specialties tied to a trademark. Each edge is associated with a given trademark.
- **Flaws**: descriptors that cause trouble.
- **3 hit boxes** (section 7).
- **8 stress boxes** (section 8).

**Creation**: choose one trademark from each category. Choose 2 edges from your trademarks' suggestions, or write your own. Choose 2 flaws, from any of the lists or written. Any trademark, edge, or flaw may be renamed to fit the concept.

The suggested edges and flaws are prompts, not a defined skill list. Interpret them through the character.

One flaw is the character's **vice**, marked on the sheet. A vice must be an indulgence: something the character does for relief. *Owes the Union* cannot be a vice. *Drinks to forget* can. Common vices: drink, stims, gambling, the feeds, luxury, devotion.

The Past, Present, and Perk trademark lists are on the **Tags** page.

---

## 2. The Action Roll

When a character attempts something risky, the GM states position and effect (section 5). Then:

1. The player invokes a single Trademark tag and any relevant Edges or Flaws under that Trademark. and rolls 1d6 per invoked tag. A tag may be invoked if it plausibly helps the action. The GM arbitrates. There is no limit on the number of invocations.
2. If no tags are invoked, roll 2d6 and keep the lowest. A 0-tag roll cannot crit.
3. Add bonus dice, if any (section 3).
4. The GM adds 1d8 per thorn (section 4).
5. Roll all dice at once.

Read the highest d6:

| Highest d6 | Result |
|---|---|
| Two or more 6s | Critical: succeed with increased effect |
| 6 | Full success: you do it |
| 4–5 | Mixed success: you do it, with a consequence |
| 1–3 | Failure: it goes wrong; consequence; you don't get what you wanted |

Then apply **cuts**: each d8 showing 7 or 8 cuts the result down one level. Cuts stack and affect criticals.

A result cut below failure is a **critical failure**: failure, with a consequence one severity step worse than position indicates. Resisting it costs 1 additional stress.

*Optional rule: if uncapped invocations slow the table down, cap invocations at one trademark plus one edge per roll.*

---

## 3. Bonus Dice

Each of the following may be used once per roll:

- **Push yourself**: take 2 stress for +1d.
- **Assist**: an ally states how they help, takes 1 stress, and grants +1d. The ally can suffer the roll's consequences if the fiction warrants.
- **Devil's bargain**: accept a complication for +1d. Anyone at the table may propose a bargain; the GM approves; the roller may decline. The complication occurs regardless of the roll's result and cannot be resisted. Bargains usually activate a flaw.

---

## 4. Thorns

Thorns represent facts that make the attempt less likely to succeed. Each thorn adds 1d8 to the roll.

**The thorn test**: if a fact makes the attempt less likely to succeed, it adds a thorn. If it reduces what a success accomplishes, it reduces effect instead (section 5). Each fact counts once, in one lane.

Thorn sources:

- Circumstances: darkness, a moving target, decompression, time pressure, heat at 6 or higher.
- Opposing tags that impede the attempt: *Ace Pilot* against your escape.
- The character's own conditions and flaws, when the fiction turns them against the character.

Effect reduction, not thorns:

- Quality or scale that limits what success achieves: *Military-Grade Hull* makes a ship harder to hurt, not harder to hit.

The lane depends on the stated approach. Example: shooting an armored ship is a normal shot at reduced effect. A called shot at an exposed heat vent bypasses the armor for full effect, and the small target adds a thorn.

Most rolls have 0 to 2 thorns. Three or more represents extreme circumstances.

---

## 5. Position and Effect

The GM states both before the roll.

- **Position** sets consequence severity: controlled, risky, or desperate.
- **Effect** sets progress on a success: limited (1 clock segment), standard (2), or great (3). A critical adds one effect level.

Thorns change neither position nor effect. Position and effect can be traded against each other before the roll.

---

## 6. Consequences and Resistance

Consequences include: harm (section 7), a complication (heat, a clock ticks, reinforcements arrive), lost opportunity, worse position, or asset strain (section 9). Severity follows position. Critical failures escalate severity one step.

**Resistance**: describe how the character resists a consequence, then roll a pool from tags that plausibly protect them (0 applicable tags: 2d6 keep lowest). Take stress equal to 6 minus the highest die. On a critical, clear 1 stress instead. The GM reduces or negates the consequence.

Critical failure consequences cost 1 additional stress to resist. Devil's bargain complications cannot be resisted.

---

## 7. Harm

Characters have 3 hit boxes. Consequences deal hits by position: controlled 0–1, risky 1, desperate 2. Desperate-severity consequences and critical failures also inflict a **condition**.

Conditions are temporary flaw tags written on the sheet: *Cracked Ribs, Concussed, Rattled.* They add thorns when relevant and can be targets of devil's bargains. Conditions never grant flaw XP (section 14).

A character with all hit boxes marked is taken out. The form this takes follows the fiction and position: unconscious, captured, adrift, dying.

**Recovery**: a downtime action with adequate facilities clears all hit boxes. Each condition has a 4-segment recovery clock. A recovery roll (pool from relevant tags, such as a medic or a medbay) ticks the clock by the rolled effect. Severe conditions downgrade to lesser conditions before clearing.

---

## 8. Stress, Vice, and Trauma

Characters have 8 stress boxes. Stress is spent to push (2), assist (1), flashback (0–2), and resist (variable).

**Flashbacks**: declare something the character prepared earlier. Cost: 0 stress for ordinary preparation, 1 for complex, 2 for elaborate. Roll if the flashback action was chancy.

**Trauma**: when the stress track fills, clear it. The character exits the scene (flees, freezes, collapses, lashes out) and gains a permanent flaw tag: *Cold, Reckless, Haunted, Paranoid.* Trauma tags grant flaw XP like other flaws, can never be rewritten, and can never be vices. At 4 trauma tags, the character retires.

**Indulging your vice** (downtime action): roll 1d6 and clear stress equal to the result. You may roll +1d and keep the highest; this increases the risk below. If you clear more stress than you had marked, you overindulge: the vice flaw activates as a complication (heat, a debt, an absence).

A character always has exactly one vice. If a long-term project rewrites the vice flaw, the vice marker moves to the rewritten flaw or to another flaw of the player's choice.

---

## 9. Assets

Ships, bases, and similar group assets have trademarks, edges, and flaws, like characters.

> **ICV Long Haul** — Trademarks: *Engine Room, Smuggler's Holds.* Edges: *Warp Drive, Torpedoes.* Flaws: *Held Together With Duct Tape, Registered to a Dead Man.*

Tag only what is significant to the story. A ship without an *Engine Room* trademark still has an engine.

Asset tags are invoked like any tag and add to the same pool as personal tags.

**Asset strain**: when an asset tag was invoked and the roll goes wrong, the asset is the default target of the consequence.

- On a failure or critical failure: the asset is expended or offline (torpedoes spent, drive scrammed, system jammed) until restocked or repaired.
- On a mixed success: the asset is strained. It works this time. The GM may attach a temporary flaw (*Coolant Leak*) or declare it has one use left.
- Consumables deplete. Installed systems jam or gain temporary flaws.
- Strain is part of the roll's consequence, not an addition to it. It can be resisted like any consequence.

**Repair and restock**: a downtime action plus whatever payment the fiction requires. Asset flaws activate as bargains or thorns when systems are pushed past their rating.

The ship's sheet also carries the heat track, wanted level, cred, debts, and upgrade clocks. Upgrades are bought with cred and long-term projects, not XP.

---

## 10. NPCs and Factions

NPCs and factions are lists of tags.

- Their tags that **impede a PC's attempt** become thorns; their tags that **blunt what a success accomplishes** reduce effect — the thorn test (section 4), applied per declared action.
- For a fortune roll made on their behalf, their pool is their relevant tags.
- A faction's tier is approximately its tag count.
- Factions have goal clocks, ticked in play and by fortune rolls between sessions.

---

## 11. Fortune Rolls

For outcomes not driven by a PC action (off-screen faction moves, weather, luck), use the same procedure: 1d6 per favorable fact, 1d8 per opposing fact, read the ladder, apply cuts. No position or resistance applies.

---

## 12. Scores

1. **The job**: a faction wants something, and an obstacle is in the way. One sentence.
2. **The approach**: players choose a plan type (assault, stealth, social, transport, salvage) and answer one detail question. Nothing else is planned; flashbacks cover preparation.
3. **Engagement roll** (a fortune roll): 1d6 per favorable factor (solid intel, an inside contact, crew reputation), 1d8 per opposing factor (the target's relevant tags, alert status). The result sets the opening position:

| Result | Opening |
|---|---|
| Critical | Controlled, plus an advantage |
| Full | Controlled |
| Mixed | Risky |
| Failure | Desperate |
| Critical failure | Desperate, and a threat clock starts ticking |

4. Cut directly to the action.

**Payoff**: cred and rep per the job.

**Heat**: 0–2 for a quiet job. +2 if loud, +2 if there were killings, +2 for a high-profile target, +2 on hostile turf. At 8 heat, clear the track and increase wanted level by 1.

**Entanglement roll** after each job (a fortune roll; wanted level adds thorns):

| Result | Entanglement |
|---|---|
| Critical | An old favor comes due, in your favor |
| Full | Routine inspection; talk or pay your way through |
| Mixed | Union trouble, a debt called in, or a rival crew poaching a contact |
| Failure | A bounty hunter, cargo seizure, or a stowaway with a problem |
| Critical failure | The thing you stole calls home |

## 13. Downtime

Each character takes two downtime actions between jobs. Additional actions cost cred.

Actions: **recover** (section 7), **reduce heat** (a fortune roll; clear heat equal to effect), **indulge vice** (section 8), **repair or restock** (section 9), **acquire** (borrow, rent, or rig a temporary asset for the next job), **long-term project** (a clock toward any goal: retrofit a sensor suite, cultivate a fixer, forge a registry).

Long-term projects grant fictional permission and temporary tags. A temporary tag lasts one job per maintenance action spent on it. Permanent tags are gained only through XP.

**Taking on a flaw** (free, costs no action): during downtime, a player may add a flaw to their character if it is grounded in events. The GM has final say. A new flaw must be capable of causing the character trouble.

---

## 14. Advancement

Mark 1 XP when:

- An action roll results in failure or critical failure.
- You act in a desperate position, regardless of outcome.

At the end of each session, mark 1 XP for each yes:

- Did you take a big risk for a crewmate?
- Did you have an emotionally charged scene with another PC?
- Did the job show the hardships of the crew's life?

**Flaw tally**: at the end of each session, each permanent flaw (including trauma tags) that created a significant problem grants 1 XP, at most once per flaw per session. A problem is significant if it inflicted a concrete cost: a consequence, heat, a ticked clock, or a lost opportunity. Devil's bargain complications count. Conditions never grant flaw XP.

**Spending XP**: a new edge costs the current slot price. The first slot costs 4 XP; each subsequent slot costs 2 more (4, 6, 8, 10, and so on). A new trademark costs double the current slot price and uses one slot.

Flaws are never bought off. A long-term project can rewrite a flaw into a different flaw. Trauma tags cannot be rewritten. If the vice flaw is rewritten, the vice marker moves (section 8).

---

## 15. Group Actions and Setup

**Group action**: each participant rolls their own pool. The best single result stands. Roll the thorn dice once and apply cuts to that result. The leader takes 1 stress for each participant whose own roll was a failure or worse.

**Setup**: a successful setup action grants improved position or improved effect to those who follow through on it.

---

## 16. Running the Game

- Each fictional fact counts once: as a die, a thorn, an effect modifier, or position. Never two.
- To set thorns, list the relevant facts and apply the thorn test to each. Do not estimate a difficulty number.
- A flaw earns XP only through real cost. Do not award flaw XP for cosmetic activations.
- Prep consists of: a faction list with tags and one clock each, a job seed per faction, and the current port.

## Appendix A: Probabilities

Chance of a mixed success or better, before considering criticals:

| Pool | 0 thorns | 1 thorn | 2 thorns | 3 thorns |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 tags | 25% | 21% | 17% | 14% |
| 1d | 50% | 42% | 34% | 28% |
| 2d | 75% | 64% | 54% | 45% |
| 3d | 88% | 76% | 65% | 55% |
| 4d | 94% | 83% | 72% | 61% |

Each thorn has a 1-in-4 chance of cutting the result one level.

## Appendix B: Roll Procedure

1. Say what you do. The GM states position and effect.
2. Invoke tags: 1d6 each. No tags: 2d6, keep the lowest.
3. Optional, once each: push (2 stress), assist (helper takes 1 stress), devil's bargain.
4. The GM adds 1d8 per thorn: every fact that makes the attempt less likely to succeed. Facts that blunt what success accomplishes reduce effect instead.
5. Roll everything. Highest d6: 1–3 failure, 4–5 mixed, 6 full, two or more 6s critical.
6. Each d8 showing 7 or 8 cuts the result one level. Below failure is a critical failure.
7. Apply the outcome. Consequences may be resisted: stress equals 6 minus the highest die.
8. If an asset tag was invoked and the roll went wrong, the asset strains.
